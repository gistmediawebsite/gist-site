Every day from April to September, more than 17 homes priced at ₹5 crore or above were registered in the Mumbai region. That's not a boom headline. That's a Tuesday.

The numbers come from *The Luxury Real Estate Market Decoded*, a half-yearly report by Lighthouse Luxury and property-registration platform Zapkey, built on registered transactions. Business Standard, Business Today and Free Press Journal all reported it this week.

## The headline numbers

- **3,145 luxury homes** sold in April–September 2026, worth **₹32,623 crore**
- **July** was the peak month: 618 homes worth ₹6,669 crore
- July–September deals rose **10.5%** over the previous quarter to 1,651; their value rose **17.1%** to ₹17,600 crore
- The average luxury ticket rose 6% to **₹10.7 crore**

About seven in ten deals were in the "entry" ₹5–10 crore band. Homes above ₹20 crore brought in more than a quarter of the money.

## The ₹50 crore club

This is where it gets interesting. Homes priced at ₹50 crore and above went from **9 deals in April–June to 20 in July–September**. September alone had nine, matching the entire first quarter. Over six months, 29 such homes sold for ₹2,536 crore combined.

At NamanXana, three homes went for a combined ₹456 crore, an average of ₹152 crore each.

"Confidence at the very top is back," said Sumesh Mishra, founder of Lighthouse Luxury.

## Where the money is going

Worli is still the capital of Mumbai money: 275 deals worth ₹5,558 crore, an average of ₹20.2 crore per home and about 17% of all luxury value. Godrej Trilogy Seafront in Worli was the top project at ₹1,199 crore of sales.

But the map is widening. Andheri West and Versova doubled in value in a single quarter, and Tardeo grew 125%. By developer, Godrej Properties led with ₹2,360 crore across 207 homes, followed by Oberoi Realty (₹1,818 crore) and Lodha (₹1,741 crore).

## And if you'd rather rent

Between June and September, eight Mumbai homes were leased at ₹20 lakh a month or more, seven of them to companies. The top one, at Oberoi 360 West in Worli, rents for **₹28.5 lakh a month**, or ₹3.42 crore a year before stamp duty and fees. That building holds three of the top eight leases, as per Business Standard.

## Why it matters

India's wealth story is usually told in stock indices and IPO subscriptions. Mumbai's land registry tells it more bluntly: the people at the top are buying, in nine-figure deals, and demand is spreading beyond a single postcode. With festive registrations still to be counted, Mishra told Business Standard he expects the second half to build on the first. The rest of us can keep refreshing the listings.

*Figures are from registered transactions compiled by Lighthouse Luxury and Zapkey, and may be revised.*
