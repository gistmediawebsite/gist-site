Kimi Antonelli needs two points tonight. Most Formula 1 drivers spend whole careers chasing a title. He needs ninth place.

The 20-year-old Mercedes driver goes into the Mexico City Grand Prix 123 points clear of teammate George Russell, with only 125 points left in the season. Lights out is 2pm local time on Sunday, which is 1:30am on Monday in India. Set an alarm.

## How Austin set it up

Antonelli won the United States Grand Prix on 25 October from pole to chequered flag. It was his ninth win in 18 races this season. Per Autosport, he finished 13.078 seconds clear of Charles Leclerc's Ferrari, with Max Verstappen third and Lewis Hamilton fourth.

His title rivals did not help themselves. Russell, handed a five-second penalty on the first lap according to Autosport, came home eighth, just enough to stay mathematically alive.

"The car felt perfect today," Antonelli said afterwards, according to AP. Leclerc, asked about plans for a title party, was already looking ahead: "So probably Mexico, then?" Verstappen's suggestion, per the same report: "Some tequila."

## The maths for tonight

Only Russell and Hamilton can still catch him. Here is what Antonelli needs at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez:

- **Finish ninth or better:** champion, whatever anyone else does.
- **If Hamilton wins:** tenth place is enough.
- **If neither Russell nor Hamilton wins:** champion, even with no points at all.

Put simply, the party is postponed only if Russell wins and Antonelli finishes tenth or lower, or Hamilton wins and Antonelli finishes outside the top ten. Clinch it tonight and the last four rounds, including the Gulf double-header, become a lap of honour.

## The records waiting

Win it and Antonelli becomes the youngest world champion in F1 history, beating the mark Sebastian Vettel set at 23 in 2010. He would also be Italy's first drivers' champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953, ending a 73-year wait for a country that treats F1 as a second religion.

## What could get in the way

Mexico City sits about 2,200 metres above sea level, the highest track on the calendar, and thin air makes cars harder to cool and harder to read, Formula1.com notes. Leclerc topped Friday's second practice session, with Antonelli fourth. Verstappen has won here five times, per TSN, and Formula1.com counts six straight top-three finishes for him.

There is also a home hero in the mix: Sergio Perez makes his 300th F1 start, now with Cadillac.

## Why it matters

A teenager when the season started, potentially a champion before the Gulf double-header: Antonelli's rise has been the story of 2026. If he finishes the job tonight, Mercedes have a new face for a decade and Italy finally gets its driver. If he doesn't, he will have to settle for a near-certain title with four races left. Hard life.
