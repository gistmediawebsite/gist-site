Last season he was the rookie many said Mercedes had promoted too early. Today Andrea Kimi Antonelli leads the Formula 1 world championship by 84 points, and the only real question is when, not whether.

## The numbers

After 16 of 23 rounds, Antonelli has 320 points and eight wins. His Mercedes teammate George Russell is second on 236. Behind them the field is bunched: Lewis Hamilton 214, Charles Leclerc 191, then defending champion Lando Norris and Max Verstappen level on 188.

Mercedes, meanwhile, lead the constructors' table on 556 points, 151 clear of Ferrari. The team that built 2026's car best is running away with both trophies.

## The maths of the run-in

There are 175 drivers' points still available across seven Grands Prix and one sprint. Five drivers besides Antonelli are still mathematically alive. Realistically, it's Russell's to chase: he needs to outscore his own teammate by an average of 12 points a weekend just to draw level by Abu Dhabi.

The rule of thumb for the clinch is straightforward. Once Antonelli's lead is bigger than the points left on the table, it's over. If his current form holds, that moment could arrive before the Abu Dhabi finale on 6 December.

## The record

Sebastian Vettel has held the mark for the youngest world champion since 2010, when he won the title at 23. Antonelli turned 20 in August. If he closes this out, he won't just beat the record. He'll take three years off it.

## Why it matters off the track

A 20-year-old Italian champion in a silver Mercedes is the kind of story that sells grandstands for a decade. Expect Abu Dhabi hospitality, already almost sold out, to become the most-asked-for ticket in the GIST inbox.
