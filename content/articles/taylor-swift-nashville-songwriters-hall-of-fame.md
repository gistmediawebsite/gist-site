The biggest pop star on the planet went back to the town where she used to carry her guitar into writers' rooms, and the town gave her its highest honour.

On Monday 26 October, Taylor Swift was inducted into the **Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame** at its 56th anniversary gala at Music City Center. At 36, she is the youngest person the hall has ever inducted, as reported by Country Now, Deseret News and Taste of Country.

## The night

Swift went in under the songwriter/artist category. Brad Paisley, who took her on tour early in her career, did the introduction.

"This is somebody who has conquered the world and done it with songs," Paisley said. He also called her "the voice of the generation."

She joined a class that included Lyle Lovett, Bruce Channel, Shawn Camp, Lee Thomas Miller and, posthumously, J.I. Allison. George Strait was in the room to accept an honour of his own. As per Deseret News, Lainey Wilson performed "Love Story" as part of the tribute.

## The speech

Swift spoke for nearly 10 minutes, per Taste of Country, and kept it about the craft rather than the charts.

"I knew from the time I wrote my first song that even if it couldn't be my career, it would be my passion," she said.

She called Nashville "the place that I went to in my middle school daydreams," and credited it with teaching her discipline: "I learned by repetition. I think you truly learn a craft when you do it over and over and over."

She also opened with a joke at Paisley's expense, telling him she was surprised to see him "because we've been estranged since 2007." Twenty years of friendship, apparently, still leaves room for one good roast.

## Twenty years, two halls

The timing is neat. Swift moved to Nashville as a young teenager and, according to Taste of Country, signed with Sony's publishing arm at 14. Her self-titled debut album came out in October 2006, so it turned 20 in the same week as the induction.

It's also her second songwriting hall of fame in 2026. In January, the New York-based **Songwriters Hall of Fame** named her to its 2026 class, with a ceremony on 11 June. That hall requires a writer's first commercial release to be at least 20 years old; her debut single "Tim McGraw" reached radio in June 2006. Stevie Wonder, inducted at 32 in 1983, remains its youngest member, according to Variety and NBC New York.

## Why it matters

Swift's career is usually discussed in records broken and stadiums sold out. Two songwriting halls of fame in one year say the quiet part out loud: the songs are the point. For anyone who writes, in Nashville or Nagpur, the message from her speech is the unglamorous one. You get good by doing it over and over and over.
