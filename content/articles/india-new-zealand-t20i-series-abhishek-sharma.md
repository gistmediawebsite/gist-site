New Zealand made 198 in Wellington and lost with 40 balls to spare. That is not a defeat. That is an audit.

India's young T20 side has spent the last ten days turning a five-match series in New Zealand into a highlights reel. They won the first three games to seal the series 3-0 with two to play, before the Black Caps finally landed a punch at Eden Park.

## The Wellington demolition

On 27 October, Daryl Mitchell's unbeaten 89 off 52 balls, his best T20I score, took New Zealand to 198 for 3. A good total on most days. Not this one.

Abhishek Sharma and Vaibhav Sooryavanshi, promoted to open ahead of Sanju Samson, took 26 off the first over and 118 off the six-over powerplay. That is the highest powerplay score by any team in men's T20Is. Their opening stand was worth 133.

Sooryavanshi made 45 off 19. Abhishek reached his hundred in 35 balls, the fastest T20I century against New Zealand, and was out for 113 off 38. India got home in 13.2 overs.

Only one chase of 190-plus has ever finished faster: Estonia against Cyprus in 2024, with 42 balls left. India's 40 is second on that list, per the ICC and Outlook India.

"Pretty tough to stop those guys when they're going," said New Zealand captain Mitchell Santner. Fair.

## How India got there

The series opened in Christchurch on 22 October with India chasing 226, their highest successful T20I chase. Glenn Phillips had made 82 not out off 40, but Samson (67 off 33) and Naman Dhir (42 not out off 15) got India home with two balls left under captain Shreyas Iyer.

Two days later, at the same ground, Santner took 4 for 21 and India slipped to 71 for 5 chasing 181. Tilak Varma (51 not out off 26) and Nitish Kumar Reddy (26 not out off 13) dragged them over the line with one ball to spare.

## Then Auckland happened

On 30 October, India won the toss, bowled, and watched New Zealand pile up 259 for 7. India were held to 172 for 9 and lost by 87 runs. According to 1News, Phillips smashed 107 off 49 balls.

It was a reminder that Eden Park's short boundaries are generous to whoever is batting, and that New Zealand had pushed India to the final over in both Christchurch games.

## What's next

The fifth and final T20I was played in Hamilton on Sunday, where India batted first and made 191/8. Then the tour keeps going: five ODIs from 4 November and two Tests in Wellington and Christchurch, ending on 1 December. New Zealand Cricket calls this 12-match visit the largest inbound tour it has ever hosted.

## Why it matters

India's T20 future has arrived early, and it has no interest in settling in. This side won a series away from home by playing at a tempo New Zealand could not match for three straight games. The Auckland hammering keeps everyone honest. The ODIs will show whether the depth runs as deep as the strike rate.
