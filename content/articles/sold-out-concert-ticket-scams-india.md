Every big India show now runs on the same script: the official sale opens, the inventory vanishes in minutes, and the "I have 2 extra, DM me" posts appear before the queue has even cleared.

This winter's calendar, with Diljit Dosanjh's seven-city tour, Guns N' Roses, Fred again.., The Chainsmokers and more, means the scammers are busy too.

## This has already gone to the Enforcement Directorate

In October 2024 the ED searched 13 locations across Delhi, Mumbai, Jaipur, Chandigarh and Bengaluru in a money-laundering probe into black-market and fake tickets for Coldplay's India shows and Diljit Dosanjh's Dil-Luminati tour. Its press release said people had been selling tickets, including fakes, through Instagram, WhatsApp and Telegram. Phones, laptops and SIM cards were seized. Some Dil-Luminati tickets were reportedly black-marketed for more than ₹85,000.

The same week, the Delhi High Court asked the Centre to respond to a petition seeking a framework for regulated ticket resale.

## Five rules before you pay anyone

1. **Know the official seller.** For Diljit's Aura India tour, that's BookMyShow. If the seller isn't the platform named by the promoter, assume it's a problem.
2. **Check whether tickets are transferable at all.** Many aren't. Tomorrowland Thailand passes are tied to buyer accounts and delivered as personalised bracelets. Wimbledon's conditions let it cancel tickets resold outside its own channels.
3. **Never pay by UPI to an individual for a "ticket screenshot".** A screenshot can be sold ten times.
4. **Use the official resale or waitlist** if the event has one. It's slower, but it's the only resale that's guaranteed to scan.
5. **If it's urgent, it's a scam.** "Only 2 left, pay in 10 minutes" is a pressure tactic, not inventory.

## And if you've already been cheated

File a complaint on the national cybercrime portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or call 1930. Do it quickly: the faster a complaint is logged, the better the chance a transaction can be frozen.
